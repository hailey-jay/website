title: Drawing the snake lemma standfirst: A walkthrough of the editor, from four bullets to a connecting morphism. description: Recreating the snake lemma in Sagitta, key by key: rows of objects, rectangle select, control points, and an arrow that crosses over.

I quite like the wikipedia snake lemma figure. Let's recreate it in Sagitta.

I'd first start by making each row of the diagram. SPC on an empty cell creates a vertex. If a vertex is already selected, an arrow is drawn from the previous vertex to the current cell. Pressing SPC and → alternately a few times gives a sequence of bullets. Let's make four:

[image seq.png | Four bullets in a row. | Sagitta screenshot: Commutative diagram: bullet, mapping right to bullet, mapping right to bullet, mapping right to bullet.]

We could either fill in their labels now or wait until later. One of the ways we could do this is to hit TAB a bunch of times, which cycles through the unlabelled vertices and arrows so that you can edit their labels. If we didn't use the queue feature, we could select arrows for labelling by / plus whatever code the arrow got assigned (usually, the letters of their endpoints concatenated). Here, the code that got assigned to the arrow we need to label g got assigned CODE

[image search.png | Selecting an arrow by its code. | Sagitta screenshot: Demonstrating search functionality.]

After, let's escape out of the selection and build the rest of the objects in rows. When it's time to connect the rows together, we can just clear our selection, press SPC on the source vertex, and then SPC on the target. If we loaded a custom macros.tex file, we could've defined \coker, but for this quick example, something fast and dirty will do. With most everything but the connecting homomorphism done, we have:

[image grid.png | Both rows, joined, with the kernels and cokernels in sequence. | Sagitta screenshot: Commutative diagram: two exact rows, A by f to B to C by g to 0, then 0 to A' by f' to B' by g' to C'. Connected: A by a to A', B by b to B', C by c to C. Each of those morphisms is put in sequence: ker a to A by a to A' to coker a, ker b to B by b to B' to coker b, ker c to C by c to C' to coker c.]

Because we want our connecting homomorphism d to cut right through the middle of the diagram, we should move half the diagram down to clear some space. The way I'm going to select the seven lower vertices is by rectangle select: C-SPC on one corner, C-SPC on another, everything in between collected:

[image select_bottom.png | Rectangle select, from corner to corner. | Sagitta screenshot: the commutative diagram as previous, but the second sequence and the cokernels being selected.]

You could, of course, use / with a space-separated list, or even hold Shift and just move your cursor over each cell. Once everything is selected, holding Control and pressing the down arrow moves the whole selection down:

[image move_bottom.png | The lower half moved down, clearing a lane through the centre. | Sagitta screenshot: the commutative diagram as previous, but the second sequence and cokernels have been moved down for more space.]

Now, for the trickiest morphism of them all! Just creating an arrow the usual way between ker c and coker a isn't enough, as it'll just cut across the diagram centre inelegantly. Create the arrow and, with the arrow still selected, move the cursor two cells right of ker c. Hitting k creates a control point for the arrow (pressing k again would remove it).

[image start_curve.png | The first control point, bending the arrow out of the way. | Sagitta screenshot: the same commutative diagram as previous, now with an arrow from ker c to coker a partially snaking around the diagram.]

I'll go ahead and create the rest of the control points we want.

[image curve_done.png | The connecting morphism, routed the long way round. | Sagitta screenshot: the same commutative diagram as previous, the arrow from ker c to coker a now routed all the way around the diagram through its control points.]

In the original wikipedia image, the connecting morphism lies overtop the arrows in the middle. With the connecting morphism still selected, we can just hit x to let it cross over:

[image curve_cut.png | Crossing over, cutting a gap in the arrows beneath. | Sagitta screenshot: the same commutative diagram as previous, the morphism now cutting through the centre overlaying the arrows a, b, c and hiding the labels.]

Nearly there, but the labels are all wonky: a, b, and c are hidden underneath the connecting morphism! Select each arrow in turn, hit P, and shift the label position along the arrow using the arrow keys. Do the same for the connecting morphism, and make sure the label renders along the outside of the arrow by hitting A for label alignment and then r for right. Toggle the grid with # and enjoy!

[image complete.png | The finished diagram. | Sagitta screenshot: Commutative diagram: two exact rows, A by f to B to C by g to 0, then 0 to A' by f' to B' by g' to C'. Connected: A by a to A', B by b to B', C by c to C. Each of those morphisms is put in sequence: ker a to A by a to A' to coker a, ker b to B by b to B' to coker b, ker c to C by c to C' to coker c. A connecting morphism d snakes from ker c to coker a, laying overtop the centre of the diagram to avoid visual clutter.]

I'll just save it to a file with C-s and put it in my very cool paper on the snake lemma.

There's more complete documentation in the manual.

This walkthrough was written against Sagitta v0.4.1. There's not even a major version out yet, so a lot is still subject to change.
