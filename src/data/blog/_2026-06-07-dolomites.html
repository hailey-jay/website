Hey yall!

This is shaping up to be a bit of a longer blog as I catalogue my trip to Dobbiaco/Toblach. I'm here for summer school and I anticipate a lot is going to happen, although by this point, I've barely experienced anything.

Friday, May 29 - Saturday, May 30. Baton Rouge to Dallas to Mestre: Even a 5:00 wakeup isn't early enough to watch the undergrads I've been mentoring present. I'll probably post about them soon enough; I'm deeply proud of all they've done and all they're sure to do.

Security was remarkably fast and there was hardly anyone in line. It was a surprisingly painless experience! A win for BTR. The flight to DFW was quick and painless. Sadly, the layover wasn't nearly as friendly. After having overpriced and extremely sad drunken noodles (hey, it's food), I boarded the flight to VCE. This flight would turn out to be a bit harder.

For one, I don't love flying. I get a little planesick and it's a tiring experience. For two, this flight is the longest I've been on, clocking in at around 10 hours. For three, this is the first time I've ever been out of the country. To avoid all of these issues, I attempted to sleep. This was interrupted by a passenger collapsing somewhere over the Atlantic (she's alright) and subsequently a mislabeled 'comedy' film that turned out to be horrific and disgusting. I skipped the in-flight meal, coasting off of the ever-more-good-sounding drunken noodles. At last, the flight was over. I collected my things, hurried through the gate and customs, and then, I was in Italy.

Saturday, May 30. Mestre: If you're keeping track, it's about noon at this point. I've surpassed 24 hours awake and it's starting to show; even turning my head makes my vision swirl. It's too late to check into my hotel, so I wander for a little bit.

The city feels strangely like New Orleans, if New Orleans were more bike-friendly. There's litter on the ground, shady trees covering the street, artsy cinemas, decaying buildings, 30°… everything a NOLA gal needs to feel at home. I did note the amount of political posters and antifascist graffiti I saw; that was something different. I will not discuss my politics here as I feel that would be inappropriate.

Eventually, I found somewhere to eat, a ramen place with some stellar onigiri. I also ordered a pork bowl which eventually became my dinner. Big shoutout to PLACEHOLDER FOR IMAGE AND RESTAURANT. And yes, I did go all the way to Italy just to have Japanese food; it's just so much safer with my allergies (this is foreshadowing). After a delicious lunch, I made my way back to my hostel. Struggling against the ticking clock and ever encroaching exhaustion, I set my alarm, tried to get some work done, and, succeeding in my quest to emerge from my battle with jetlag victorious, went to bed at 21:00.

Sunday, May 31. Mestre to Dobbiaco: I desperately needed a good night's rest, and I got uncharacteristically lucky. I went to the lobby to grab a shot of unsweetened espresso, chatted with a nice tourist from New Zealand, and went to the bus station to wait.

The bus ride was absolutely gorgeous. After a week of travel anxiety, I really needed to see mountains. The Rockies are absolutely gorgeous but the Dolomites do challenge them! Unfortunately, I got bus-sick and spent most of the three hour ride with my head against the window while taking slow breaths. The hotel and town at large were gorgeous. I felt comforted. I ended up skipping dinner (mostly because I didn't know they were providing dinner that night) and fell asleep quickly.

Monday, June 1. Dobbiaco: I met some nice folks in the morning and they invited me hiking. Electing to ignore the fact that I hadn't really eaten much, I agreed. I realized that I had made a mistake the moment one revealed she had just completed a marathon. Fortunately, at the top of the trail at our destination, I was able to eat an absolutely delicious strudel. Unfortunately, there was no bus at our destination, so we had to hike down. Needless to say, I missed the first talk of the summer school. That evening, however, I was treated to what feels to my mind a banquet: A salad, followed by pasta, then a venison burger, and finally, another strudel. I was deeply thankful for the energy and I slept soundly.
